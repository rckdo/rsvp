Before you go

Timing matters more than anything else on this page. With the ceremony on 21 September, aim to choose by mid-August: made-to-order rings typically take 3 to 6 weeks, engraving adds 1 to 2 more, and you want them home with time to check the fit. Off-the-shelf in a standard size can be same-day, so there is a fallback either way.

Most Jewellery Quarter shops close on Sundays and many on Mondays; Saturday is the big day. Ring (pun intended) ahead for anywhere you are making a special trip for. Listings below are a starting point, not gospel: check names and hours before travelling.

Where to buy

The Quarter makes around 40% of the UK's jewellery, so you are shopping at the source: trade prices, on-site workshops, and people who make rings rather than just sell them.

The Vyse Street cluster

The dense stretch between the Chamberlain Clock and the Jewellery Quarter station is wall-to-wall ring retailers, dozens of them, from diamond specialists to plain-band makers. The efficient plan: walk it once without buying, shortlist three, then go back.

Prices for the same spec vary noticeably between neighbouring shops, so comparing is genuinely worth the hour.

Named starting points

  • Marlows Diamonds (Vyse St): one of the biggest, strong on wedding bands alongside diamonds.
  • Charles Green & Son: historic Birmingham makers, wedding rings since the 1820s.
  • Hockley Mint: major JQ wedding-ring manufacturer; their rings are stocked by independents across the Quarter.

Make your own idea

Several JQ workshops (the Argent Centre on Frederick Street is the hub) run make-your-own-wedding-rings days: you each make the other's ring under a goldsmith's guidance. A day out, a story for the ceremony, and the rings mean more.

These book up and need lead time for hallmarking, so if it appeals, book now.

Hallmarking

UK rings over the weight thresholds must be hallmarked, and the Birmingham Assay Office, the busiest in the world, is right there in the Quarter (its mark is an anchor). Any ring bought or made in the JQ will carry it: maker's mark, fineness (750 = 18ct, 950 = platinum), anchor, and year letter.

Ring knowledge

Enough to walk in confident and not get upsold.

Symbolism

The unbroken circle, no beginning and no end, has stood for permanence since ancient Egypt. The fourth finger of the left hand comes from the Roman vena amoris, the "vein of love" once believed to run straight to the heart. Wrong anatomy, right sentiment, and two thousand years of momentum.

Your ring words at the ceremony (F or G on the tracker) say exactly this: a lasting reminder of the promises made.

Metals

  • Yellow gold: the classic; 18ct is richer in colour, 9ct is harder-wearing and cheaper.
  • White gold: rhodium-plated; expect re-plating every few years.
  • Rose gold: warm, wears well, suits vintage looks.
  • Platinum: naturally white, dense, hypoallergenic, needs no plating; the buy-once option.
  • Titanium / tungsten: cheap and tough but cannot be resized; think hard before committing.

Profile and fit

The profile is the cross-section: court (rounded inside and out, the most comfortable), D-shape (flat inside, domed out), flat (modern, squarer feel). Width from 2mm (delicate) to 6mm+ (statement); try the width with the profile, they change each other.

Get sized at the end of the day when fingers are warmest and largest, and wear it around the shop for ten minutes before deciding.

Practical checklist

  • Confirm resizing policy (free first resize is common).
  • Engraving: date or initials inside; ask the lead time.
  • Ask for the hallmark and a receipt with the spec.
  • Check it against the ring words: who carries them forward on the day is on the tracker.
  • Add them to home insurance once bought.

Food and drink nearby

Make a day of it. All within a ten-minute walk of the Chamberlain Clock.

Pubs with history

  • The Rose Villa Tavern: Victorian tiled beauty at the Vyse St / Warstone Lane corner, proper pub food.
  • 1000 Trades (Frederick St): craft beer, rotating kitchen residencies, JQ institution.
  • The Button Factory (Frederick St): big terrace, relaxed menu, good for a sunny afternoon.

Proper meals

  • Otto (Caroline St): wood-fired pizza, casual and excellent.
  • The Wilderness: tasting-menu fine dining if the ring shopping goes well enough to celebrate.
  • St Paul's Square: the Georgian square is ringed with restaurants and bars; wander and pick.

Evenings: The Jam House on the square does live music and dinner.
