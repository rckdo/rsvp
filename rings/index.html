Where to buy

Vintage first, since that's more your style, then the new-stock names as backup.

Vintage and antique your style

Vintage and Modern (29a Warstone Lane): family-run, a wide range of sensibly priced vintage and modern pieces, with restoration and repair done in-house. The closest thing in the Quarter to exactly what you're after; tap its pin on the map for live Monday hours before you commit the walk.

Beyond it, plenty of the everyday Vyse Street and Warstone Lane shops carry antique and second-hand trays alongside the new stock. Ask directly, "what do you have in vintage or pre-owned?", because the good pieces are often in the back, not the window.

Vintage buying notes: check the band's wear (thin shanks need building up), confirm it can be resized (full eternity styles often can't), and use the hallmark's year letter to date the piece exactly.

The Vyse Street cluster

The dense stretch between the Chamberlain Clock and the Jewellery Quarter station is wall-to-wall ring retailers, dozens of them, from antique dealers to plain-band makers. The efficient plan: walk it once without buying, shortlist three, then go back.

Prices for the same spec vary noticeably between neighbouring shops, so comparing is genuinely worth the hour.

New, if vintage doesn't land

  • Marlows Diamonds (16 Vyse St): one of the biggest, strong on wedding bands alongside diamonds. Open 7 days.
  • Charles Green & Son: historic Birmingham makers, wedding rings since the 1820s. Weekdays, appointment-led.
  • Hockley Mint: major JQ wedding-ring manufacturer; their rings are stocked by independents across the Quarter.

Hallmarking

UK rings over the weight thresholds must be hallmarked, and the Birmingham Assay Office, the busiest in the world, is right there in the Quarter (its mark is an anchor). Any ring bought or made in the JQ will carry it: maker's mark, fineness (750 = 18ct, 950 = platinum), anchor, and year letter.

On a vintage ring the year letter is the provenance: it tells you exactly when the ring began its story.

Ring knowledge

Enough to walk in confident and not get upsold.

Symbolism

The unbroken circle, no beginning and no end, has stood for permanence since ancient Egypt. The fourth finger of the left hand comes from the Roman vena amoris, the "vein of love" once believed to run straight to the heart. Wrong anatomy, right sentiment, and two thousand years of momentum.

Your ring words at the ceremony (F or G on the tracker) say exactly this: a lasting reminder of the promises made.

Metals

  • Yellow gold: the classic; 18ct is richer in colour, 9ct is harder-wearing and cheaper.
  • White gold: rhodium-plated; expect re-plating every few years.
  • Rose gold: warm, wears well, suits vintage looks.
  • Platinum: naturally white, dense, hypoallergenic, needs no plating; the buy-once option.
  • Titanium / tungsten: cheap and tough but cannot be resized; think hard before committing.

Profile and fit

The profile is the cross-section: court (rounded inside and out, the most comfortable), D-shape (flat inside, domed out), flat (modern, squarer feel). Width from 2mm (delicate) to 6mm+ (statement); try the width with the profile, they change each other.

Get sized at the end of the day when fingers are warmest and largest, and wear it around the shop for ten minutes before deciding.

Practical checklist

  • Confirm resizing policy (free first resize is common).
  • Engraving: date or initials inside; ask the lead time.
  • Ask for the hallmark and a receipt with the spec.
  • Check it against the ring words: who carries them forward on the day is on the tracker.
  • Add them to home insurance once bought.
